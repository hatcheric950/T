# Small-business playbook (low / no budget)

For solo founders and small businesses, motion and trust beat ad spend. These
tactics cost time, not money, and usually convert better than a thin paid budget.

## Sell to who already knows you (warmest first)

1. **Existing & past customers** — easiest sale. Offer an upgrade, renewal,
   referral bonus, or bundle. A short, personal message outperforms a campaign.
2. **Waitlist / inbound / DMs** — anyone who raised a hand. Follow up; most
   never get a second touch.
3. **Warm network** — past colleagues, local business groups, partners who serve
   the same customer but don't compete.

## Offer design

- **One clear offer, one CTA.** Confusion kills small-business conversion.
- **Reduce risk**: money-back guarantee, free trial, "first month $1", or a
  no-commitment audit/consult.
- **Honest urgency only**: a real cohort start date or limited slots — never
  fake countdowns.
- **Social proof**: 2–3 specific testimonials or before/after results do more
  than any ad budget.

## Outreach (do it right)

- Personalize the first line with something real about them.
- Lead with their problem, not your product. Short. One ask.
- B2B leads via Apollo/HubSpot — only business contacts, respect credits and
  consent. Honor unsubscribe and CAN-SPAM/GDPR.
- Track reply rate and booked calls, not sends.

## Content & local presence (compounding, free)

- Google Business Profile (for local) — reviews are the highest-ROI lever.
- Post the offer where the buyer already hangs out (niche groups, subreddits,
  local FB groups) — following each community's self-promo rules.
- Repurpose one idea into a pin, a reel, and a post rather than making three
  things from scratch.

## Cadence on a deadline

If the user has a tight goal, structure the day, but be honest it's effort, not
a guaranteed number:

1. Message warm customers/leads with a clear, time-bound offer.
2. Post the offer organically across owned channels (creative from Canva/Adobe).
3. Stand up a small paid test (PAUSED → user launches) to extend reach.
4. Follow up everyone who engaged. Follow-up is where small businesses win.

The goal of a deadline isn't to promise the dollar figure — it's to maximize
qualified shots on goal and read the results honestly.
